# **AI Fundamentals**

## Artificial Intelligence (AI)
AI is the broad field of building machines that can perform tasks that normally need human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognizing images, or making decisions. For example: Google Maps suggesting the fastest route, Siri or Alexa answering voice commands, and fraud detection systems that flag suspicious bank transactions.

## Machine Learning (ML) 
Machine Learning is a subset of AI where systems learn patterns from data instead of following hand-written rules. The more data they see, the better they get. Examples: Gmail filtering spam, Netflix and Spotify recommending what to watch or listen to next, and banks predicting whether a borrower is likely to repay a loan.

## Deep Learning 
Deep Learning is a subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers to learn very complex patterns from large amounts of data, especially images, audio, and text. Examples: face unlock on your phone, self-driving cars detecting pedestrians and traffic signs, and doctors' tools that spot tumors in X-rays and MRI scans.

## Neural Networks 
Neural Networks are the building blocks of deep learning. Inspired loosely by the brain, they consist of layers of connected "neurons" that pass signals forward, adjusting their connection strengths during training to improve accuracy. Examples: handwriting recognition when you deposit a cheque using a banking app, Google Translate converting text between languages, and voice-to-text transcription on your phone.

## Generative AI 
Gen AI is AI that creates new content, such as text, images, code, music, or video, based on patterns it learned from massive datasets. Examples: Claude and ChatGPT writing emails or explaining concepts, image tools like Midjourney or DALL·E creating artwork from a text description, and GitHub Copilot suggesting code while a developer types.
